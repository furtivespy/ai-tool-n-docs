## Prompt

Run every weekday at 6:00am Eastern (America/New_York), the hour the user picked. This is NOT the tactical chat digest. Build the printable kitchen-table page following the Kitchen table newspaper rules in memory: schedule strip, cute weather, family left column, two skinny news columns, locked fonts (Liberation Serif plus Nunito only). The masthead is always "The <NEIGHBORHOOD> Breakfast Times", with "Breakfast" in the accent color. Above the title, always include a small Nunito caps top rail: "<NEIGHBORHOOD> · SYRACUSE, N.Y." on the left, "BREAKFAST EDITION · PRICE: ___" in the center (keep it on one line with white-space: nowrap), and "READ IT WITH YOUR ___" on the right. For the price, randomly pick one or two (vary it) from One Hug, One High Five, One Fist Bump, One Silly Dance, One Kiss, joined with "&" when there are two (e.g. "PRICE: ONE FIST BUMP & ONE SILLY DANCE"), and avoid repeating yesterday's exact price. Fill the breakfast blank with one breakfast picked at random each day from Cereal, Waffle, Pancakes, Pancake Puffs, Muffin Puffs, French Toast Sticks, and avoid repeating yesterday's pick if you can tell what it was. Below the title, add an italic date line ending "All the news that's fit for <CHILD_1> & <CHILD_2>". <CHILD_1> loves the price line, so never drop it. Never show dollar amounts anywhere on the paper (tuition, bills, payments): mention the item without the amount, e.g. "<CHILD_1> tuition autopay". Family facts: read /workspace/household/watch-list.md and the open-loops ledger for current changes (e.g. cancelled events: never mention them). The watch list has <CHILD_2>'s weekly Pre-K themes (use this week's theme in the family column and occasionally a joke). News: pick a broad mix of real, current, kid-appropriate world happenings across different areas, such as science and inventions, weather and nature, sports, arts, music and theater, history and anniversaries, holidays and cultures around the world, space, and good local Syracuse news. The boys' favorite interests are an occasional seasoning, not the default. Use at most one animal or zoo story per day, and avoid repeating the same topic or source from recent days (check previous pages in the household kitchen-table folder). Always include a small joke corner (Giggle Corner) with two jokes, one pitched for a 7-year-old and one for a 4-year-old, listed WITHOUT "For <CHILD_1>"/"For <CHILD_2>" labels, and vary the joke topics. Always include a small "Daily Spark" box with one breakfast-table conversation prompt that both a 7-year-old and a 4-year-old can answer. Rotate the format (an open question, a wonder fact with a question, a would-you-rather, a mini challenge), sometimes tie it to <CHILD_2>'s weekly theme or the day's news, and don't repeat recent sparks.
